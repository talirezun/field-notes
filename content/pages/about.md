## What this is

Field Notes is the edited, topical surface of a personal knowledge graph. I have
published roughly forty long-form articles, taught this material to executive
cohorts, and shipped software as someone who is not a developer. This site is
what I know, reorganised by subject instead of by date.

I am Dr. Tali Režun. I lecture at COTRUGLI Business School, I advise organisations
on AI adoption, and I build things. The brand line is "field notes from real
builds, not vendor marketing," and this site is an extension of that, not a new
thing.

## Why it is not a blog

I already publish narrative essays on [Substack](https://talirezun.substack.com),
mirrored to [Medium](https://medium.com/@talirezun). Those are chronological and
first person. They answer what happened to me.

This is the other axis. Topical, durable, extractable. It answers how something
works. Both are needed, and this site does not replace or republish the essays.
It distils across them and links back to them.

## Where the material comes from

The raw material lives in a knowledge graph I built with
[The Curator](/the-curator), my own open-source second brain. Field Notes is the
published surface of that graph, edited down by hand.

Three rules govern what makes it here:

- **Nothing is republished.** Chapters distil across articles and link to them.
  Full article text stays where it was published.
- **Every chapter cites published work.** If a claim cannot be traced to
  something already public, it does not go on the page.
- **No numbers from memory.** Prices, benchmarks, star counts and model version
  names get verified against current sources at the time of the edit. A stale
  figure on a permanent page is worse than no figure.

## What it leaves out

I build and run commercial software. Internal architecture, infrastructure
topology and security posture for those products are not published here, and
neither are compliance claims. That is a deliberate line, not an oversight.
Products get described using what is already public and nothing more.

people's systems, and a restrictive licence would fight that.
